Price-only example: P = $1 per good and W = $0.70/hour, then P = $2 and W = $1.40/hour. The real wage remains 0.70 goods/hour. The LRAS graph’s vertical axis is a price level, not an inflation rate. Its horizontal axis is real output, not labor hours.

Shock comparisons: Reset to A = 1 and K = 100 before each separate case. Higher-productivity case: A = 1.10 with the labor-supply curve fixed. Capital case: K = 110, A = 1, labor supply fixed. Lower-productivity case: A = 0.90 with K and labor supply fixed. Current potential uses the new flexible-wage clearing point. In the sticky-wage comparison, W and P are initially fixed at their old values, so labor demand falls below labor supply at W/P = 0.70. Actual hours are demand-constrained in this illustration. This is a mechanism illustration, not a full later-topic short-run model.

Scope and use: Long-run equilibrium is defined by labor-market clearing, not a fixed calendar duration. Potential is not a physical maximum or an optimal GDP target. Zero cyclical unemployment does not exclude frictional or structural unemployment. Capital is real capital and is fixed unless a case explicitly changes it. Permanent-tax effects depend on the relative income and substitution effects.
