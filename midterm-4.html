Generated Module 4 study aid. Grounded in the professor’s Topic 2 slides, SRC-012 slides 4–7 and 18–20; aggregate-production notes SRC-082 pp.1–6; and SRC-129 review pp.3–7. Each step lists its actual source. Original course files remain unchanged. The separate generated review video was not fully audited.

Units and example: Y is real output during a period; in the generated bakery illustration, loaves per day. N is total labor hours within that day. K is a real capital stock in consistent real-capital units, represented by oven capacity, not a count of bank-account dollars. A is an efficiency index. The toy bakery abstracts from raw-material details as the course function does. Numerical values are generated, not observed bakery estimates.

Model: Y = A × K^0.3 × N^0.7. MPN = 0.7 × A × (K/N)^0.3; MPK = 0.3 × A × (N/K)^0.7. ALP = Y/N. The diagrams compute curves and labels from these same functions. Marginal products are local derivatives; a whole-hour finite gain only approximately equals the local MPN. Doubling labor uses the exact power factor 2^0.7; the displayed 62.45% growth is rounded.

Comparisons: Reset A = 1, K = 100, N = 100 before separate shocks unless a step explicitly links them. Diminishing marginal gains hold other inputs fixed; constant returns scales K and N together with A fixed. Complementarity asks how a changed input affects the other input’s marginal product.

Factor payments: Income shares require the competitive marginal-product payment assumptions. Labor earns 0.7Y and capital earns 0.3Y. The Y = 200 example uses A = 2, K = N = 100 and an explicit $1-per-loaf price. The resulting $140 and $60 divide the same $200 of output value; they are not additional GDP. This is not an assertion about every firm’s actual payroll ratio.

Source wording: SRC-082’s introduction loosely describes labor measured as real wage payments; the current lecture SRC-012 slide 4 explicitly specifies N as labor hours. This lesson follows the lecture’s hours convention consistently. The derivative formulas agree across those sources.
