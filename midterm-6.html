Model and units: A competitive firm takes W (dollars per labor hour) and P (dollars per good) as given. N is labor hours during the day, not workers. MPN and real wage W/P use output goods per hour. The initial dollar example uses W = $14/hour and P = $2/loaf. The separate generated curve calibration resets A = 10 and K = 100 consistent real-capital units. It uses Y = A K^0.3 N^0.7, MPN = 0.7 A (K/N)^0.3 and desired N = K [0.7 A/(W/P)]^(1/0.3). MPN is a local derivative, not an exact finite one-hour gain. Average productivity Y/N is distinct.

Drawing conventions: All curve points and labels come from those shared formulas; only displayed values are rounded. The wage is given to the firm. The horizontal wage line is not a household supply curve. This module does not calculate a new market wage. Separate experiments reset before changing A, K or the wage. The two-firm aggregation sum is a separate illustration, not a new production calibration.

Source caution: SRC-139 Part 2 correctly states that lower K shifts labor demand left, but an intervening wage/employment sentence contradicts its later statement that N and K go down. This lesson flags that wording conflict and uses the instructor’s production and labor-demand mechanism. The optional expansion describes the inconsistency; market supply and equilibrium are reserved for later modules.
