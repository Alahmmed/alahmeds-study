Prices and real output [M1]

π = (Pnew/Pold − 1) × 100%

P = price index; π = inflation for the stated period.

Nominal GDP = Σ ptqt
Real GDP = Σ pbaseqt

p price; q quantity; Σ sum. Real GDP holds base prices fixed.

Fixed-basket index = (costt/costbase) × 100

Hold basket quantities fixed. Index 106 → price ratio 1.06.

GDP deflator = (nominal GDP/real GDP) × 100

Divide nominal GDP by deflator/100 to obtain base-year dollars.

greal = (1+gnominal)/(1+πGDP) − 1

Decimals; approx.: real ≈ nominal growth − inflation.

SRC-011 slides 52-57; SRC-079 pp.1-5; SRC-129 p.2.

Buying power and interest [M1]

Goods bought = dollars / (dollars per good)

Fixed dollars: a higher price buys fewer goods.

re ≈ i − πe; ra ≈ i − πa

i = nominal rate; e expected; a actual. Match periods.

1+ra = (1+i)/(1+πa)

Exact; decimal rates. Surprise inflation benefits fixed-debt borrowers, hurts lenders.

SRC-011 slides 59-62; SRC-129 p.2. Exact ratio: derived.

GDP and saving [M2,5]

Y = C + I + G + NX; NX = EX − IM

C consumption; I physical investment; G purchases; EX exports; IM imports.

I: new fixed capital + homes + Δinventories

Δ change. Transfers are not G; asset trades are not new output.

Value added = output − intermediate inputs

GDP = value added = final output = spending = income. Count once.

Imported phone: C +500, NX −500 ⇒ ΔY = 0

Domestic services count separately. Identity ≠ causal prediction.

S = Y − C − G = I + NX; NX = S − I

S = national saving. All quantities are flows per period.

SRC-011 slides 34-42,47-50; SRC-081 pp.1-3; SRC-140 Q1; SRC-012 slide 13.

Employment denominators [M3]

LF = E + U; pop = E + U + outside

E employed; U jobless, searching. pop = eligible civilian non-institutionalized people age 16+.

Unemployment = U/LF × 100%

Denominator LF. Search exit lowers U and LF; lower rate need not mean more jobs.

Participation = LF/pop × 100%

Denominator pop (people), not price level P. LF includes E and U.

Employment/population = E/pop × 100%

Household: people; payroll: covered jobs. Zero cyclical can leave frictional/structural unemployment.

SRC-011 slides 25-31. pop is teaching shorthand.

Production and growth [M4-5]

Y = A × K0.3 × N0.7

Y output/period; K capital stock; N hours/period; A efficiency.

ALP = Y/N = A(K/N)0.3

ALP = output/hour. Fixed A,K: N ↑ → Y ↑, ALP ↓.

Y′/Y = (A′/A)(K′/K)0.3(N′/N)0.7

Exact growth factors; prime = new. Fixed A: scale K,N together → same scale for Y.

gY ≈ gA + 0.3gK + 0.7gN

g = growth rate. Small-change approximation; match periods and rate units. Use exact factors for large changes.

gA ≈ gY − 0.3gK − 0.7gN

TFP growth is the residual; A ≠ output/hour. Higher K/N raises ALP at fixed A.

gY/N ≈ gA + 0.3(gK − gN) ≈ gY − gN

Approximate output/hour growth. g = growth rate; match periods and rate units.

SRC-012 slides 4-8,12-15; SRC-082; SRC-129 pp.3-7. Ratios derived.

Marginal products [M4,6]

MPN = 0.7 A(K/N)0.3 = 0.7Y/N

Marginal output/hour. Local gain; whole-hour gain is approximate.

MPK = 0.3 A(N/K)0.7 = 0.3Y/K

Own input ↑ → own MP ↓. K ↑ → MPN ↑; N ↑ → MPK ↑ (other inputs fixed).

Competitive firm: MPN = w = W/P

MPN > w: hire more; MPN < w: reduce hours. Fixed A,K: w ↑ → N demanded ↓.

Labor income = 0.7Y; capital income = 0.3Y

Competitive marginal-product payments divide the same Y. A or K ↑ shifts labor demand right.

SRC-012 slides 4-7,18-20; SRC-082; SRC-129 pp.3-7.

Work reward and total hours [M7,10]

w = W/P; R = [(1−tn)/(1+tc)]w

W nominal dollars/hour; P dollars/good. w gross, R net goods/hour. t_n labor tax; t_c sales tax; decimals.

w=10: labor tax 25% → 7.5; sales tax 25% → 8

Separate cases: 10×.75 vs 10/1.25. P excludes sales tax.

Ns(w) = Σjhj(w); equal h: Ns = pop×h

At the same w, sum individual hours. pop people; h hours/person.

Nd(w*) = Ns(w*) = N*

* = equilibrium; N = total hours, not people.

SRC-012 slides 21-27,32-35; SRC-083 pp.2-8,11. Tax ratio/sum derived.

Labor changes: horizon matters [M7-10]

w ↑ → Ns ↑, PVLR fixed; PVLR ↑ → Ns ↓, w fixed

PVLR = present value of lifetime resources. Wage movement versus wealth shift; normal leisure.

Temporary A ↑: Nd →; N*, w*, Y ↑

K fixed; lifetime-resource effect negligible. ALP ↑. Temporary leisure value ↑: supply ←; N ↓, w ↑, Y ↓.

Permanent A ↑: demand →; wealth effect on supply ←

Normal leisure. N may rise, stay or fall; course predicts Y ↑. Net w ↑. Permanent ≠ temporary.

Temporary tax ↑: Ns ←; N* ↓; w* ↑; R ↓

Set lifetime-resource effect aside; A,K fixed; no offsetting transfer.

Permanent tax ↑: substitution ←; income →

Normal leisure; no offsetting transfer. Net N* can ↓, stay or ↑. R lower at exact offset.

Population ↓ → aggregate Ns ←

Remaining individual supply schedules can be unchanged.

Value of leisure ↑ → individual + aggregate supply ←

Demand fixed: N* ↓, w* ↑. Wage alone moves along supply; fixed A,K holds demand fixed.

SRC-012 slides 21-35,39,54; SRC-083 pp.2-8,11; SRC-131.

Potential output and graph axes [M11]

Y* = A K0.3(N*)0.7

N* clearing hours; Y* potential output, not a physical maximum.

Labor: W/P vertical; N horizontal

Demand slopes down; course supply up, lifetime resources fixed.

LRAS: P vertical; Y horizontal; Y = Y*

P price level. Flexible W: double P and W → same W/P,N*,Y*.

A,K or clearing N* changes → LRAS can shift

P alone: along LRAS. Sticky W can put Y below Y*. Lower clearing N* alone ≠ cyclical unemployment.

SRC-012 slides 4,8,19-29,52-59; SRC-129 p.13.

Capital and investment [M12]

P × MPKnext = p(k)(r+δ)
If P=p(k): MPKnext = r+δ

P output price; p(k) capital price. Expected future MPK, real r and depreciation δ. No spread; match periods.

K′ = (1−δ)K + I; I = K′ − (1−δ)K

K current stock; K′ next stock; I gross real investment/period; δ depreciation share. δ is the earlier d.

Net I = I − δK = K′ − K

Replacement is δK. Positive gross I can accompany a falling capital stock.

r ↓ → desired K′ ↑ → I ↑ (current K fixed)

Move along I demand, vertical r / horizontal I. Higher expected A shifts demand right. A financing spread raises cost.

SRC-143 slides 4-13; SRC-085 pp.1-7; SRC-129 p.15.

Household budget [M13]

cf = (a+y−c)(1+r)+yf

a initial net assets (debt negative); y disposable real income; c consumption; f future. All real; consume final resources.

PVLR = a+y+yf/(1+r)
PVLC = c+cf/(1+r) = PVLR

Present value: compare payments in current-period units. Match the real rate to the period.

MU(c) = β(1+r)MU(cf)

MU marginal utility; β weight on future utility. Same utility and β(1+r)=1 give c=c_f.

c = cf = PVLR/[1+1/(1+r)]

Equal-consumption case; unrestricted borrowing/lending, same utility and β(1+r)=1.

s = y−c; anext = (a+s)(1+r)

s saving flow; a_next net assets after interest, before next income. Initial assets are counted once.

SRC-143 slides17-22; SRC-086; SRC-129 pp.16-25.

Income news and time [M14-15]

c = (a + Σ remaining y)/L

r=0, β=1, equal-length remaining periods, free borrowing/lending, zero final assets. L counts periods left.

MPC = Δc(today)/Δy(today)

Dollar change divided by dollar change for the stated experiment; not percentage growth.

One-time Δy today: Δc = Δy/L; MPC=1/L
Every remaining period Δy: Δc=Δy; MPC=1

Surprise changes under the equal-smoothing assumptions above. A one-time asset gain gives Δc=Δa/L.

Replan: cnew = [anow+Σ remaining expected ynew]/Lleft

a_now includes inherited saving or negative debt. Past consumption is over. Expected payday is not new news.

Keynesian c = a0 + b y; MPC=b

a0 autonomous spending, not initial assets; b current-income response. Different model from PIH.

SRC-143 slides15,22-26; SRC-133; SRC-127 extensions. Remaining-period formula: derived.

Borrowing limits [M16]

c1d = (a+y1+y2)/2

Desired equal smoothing: r=0, β=1, no final assets. a net assets available now; incomes and assets nonnegative here.

No borrowing: c1 = min(a+y1, c1d)

A borrowing rule binds only if current resources are below desired spending. Saving remains allowed.

Finite-change MPC = (cafter−cbefore)/Δy1

Use actual constrained plans. A large transfer can cross the binding threshold; its average MPC need not equal the next-dollar MPC.

SRC-128 TA clarification; SRC-143 slides28-29,31; SRC-129 pp.21-29.

IS: demand and interest [M17]

Yd = C + I(r) + G; NX=0

r vertical (real rate); Y horizontal (real output/period). Y_d is output desired by demand; need not equal Y*.

Illustration: I=I0−br
Yd=C+G+I0−br

Here C,G fixed and b>0. r as decimal; a rate change moves along IS. Changes in C,G,I0 shift it.

SRC-014 slides10-16; SRC-087. Linear equation: generated illustration.

Deficits and debt [M18]

Taxes=tnY; transfers=Tr−gY

t_n income tax rate; g transfer withdrawal rate; Tr baseline transfer term, not actual total transfers.

Dactual=G+Tr−(tn+g)Y

G purchases; D deficit flow/period. Structural D replaces actual Y with potential Y*.

Dstructural=G+Tr−(tn+g)Y*
Dcyclical=(tn+g)(Y*−Y)

Cyclical=actual−structural. Fixed policy: Y falls → taxes fall, transfers/deficit rise automatically.

Debt/GDP = B/Y ×100%

B debt stock; D deficit flow. In a simple unchanged-valuation budget: ΔB=D; debt is not the deficit.

SRC-014 slides4-8,25; SRC-147 fiscal transcript. Cyclical formula: algebraic rearrangement.

Spending multiplier [M19]

kG = ΔY/ΔG; ΔY = kGΔG

Compare with the no-policy-change outcome. Causal effect, not raw correlation during a recession.

Special illustration: kG=1/(1−m)

Constant0≤m<1 MPC; fixed real rate; unused capacity; no tax/import leakages. Not a universal measured multiplier.

SRC-014 slide26; SRC-040. Geometric-spending rule: generated derivation with stated assumptions.

Capacity and crowding out [M20]

At Y*: Y*=C+I+G; NX=0

Hold A,K,N* and C fixed in this experiment. Higher G cannot raise fixed Y*.

ΔI=−ΔG; r rises to reduce desired I

Full crowding out under these assumptions. Lower investment means lower future K; not a claim about every recession.

SRC-014 slides18-21,25; SRC-134. No fixed debt/GDP crisis threshold.

The combined-tax purchasing-power expression and individual-hour sum are explanatory accounting notation. Worked numbers are generated illustrations. The supply curve separates the current substitution effect from lifetime resources. Temporary examples use a negligible lifetime-resource effect, not an assertion that temporary income never affects wealth. Permanent tax examples assume no offsetting transfer and normal leisure. Effects are separated for explanation, not treated as successive real-world events.

The population and leisure scenarios change one factor at a time. Capital and productivity are held fixed there. The production equations explain total output versus output per labor hour; they do not establish welfare or output per person. Investment choices use expected future marginal product, a same-period real interest rate and depreciation, and equal prices for capital and output. Capital stock and investment flow are different quantities; lowercase i is the nominal interest rate, uppercase I is investment. The no-borrowing formula assumes zero interest and equal desired consumption; desired and feasible plans are different. IS measures demand-side desired output at a given real interest rate. Structural deficit uses potential output; actual deficit uses actual output. The simple multiplier and full crowding-out identities require the assumptions printed beside them.
