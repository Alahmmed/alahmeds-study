Each step gives a real locator. The generated Module 8 outline helped organize the lesson but is not an independent instructor source. Original sources remain preserved.

Numerical model: Aggregate labor demand is w = W/P = MPN = 0.7 × A × (K/N)^0.3. Illustrative supply is w = b + s × N, where b is the vertical-axis intercept and s is the slope. Baseline A = 10, K = 100, b = 2 and s = 0.05 gives N* = 100 labor hours per day and w* = 7 goods per labor hour. The supply coefficients are generated teaching choices, not estimates. Every plotted point, guide and numerical card uses these same functions; intersections are solved without rounding, then displayed to two decimals.

Units and experiments: N is total labor hours, not a count of workers. Y is real goods per day; ALP = Y/N is goods per labor hour. Separate shocks reset to the baseline. Higher A uses A = 11; lower A uses A = 9. Higher leisure value is illustrated by b = 3, not a measured or structurally derived preference response. The two-shock examples use A = 9 with b = 3 or b = 4 to demonstrate opposite wage directions under the same shift directions. Population and taxes remain fixed.

Horizon and clearing: The course treats the lifetime-resource effect of temporary wage changes as negligible. This is an explicit approximation; it does not mean temporary earnings are literally zero or that a stated windfall should be ignored. Wage flexibility and shock duration are separate assumptions. Here the wage adjusts to clear the labor market. A fixed-wage point Z compares desired quantities before that adjustment and is not the final equilibrium.

Output and productivity: Y* = A × K^0.3 × (N*)^0.7 is the current flexible-wage benchmark, not an absolute physical maximum. The exercise assumes actual output equals that clearing benchmark; no sticky-wage output gap is imposed. ALP = A × (K/N)^0.3 and competitive w* = 0.7 × ALP. Total output, output per hour and the real wage are distinct. Fewer clearing hours do not by themselves establish cyclical unemployment, a change in headcount or a welfare comparison.

Two-shock scope: SRC-012 slide 39 supports the demand-left/supply-left direction exercise. This module uses neutral temporary efficiency and leisure-value changes only. It does not teach excluded pandemic unemployment-insurance research or infer historical policy magnitudes. The combined hours result is down; the wage direction is ambiguous without relative sizes.
