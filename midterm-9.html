Model and units: This is a flexible-wage, clearing labor market. N is total labor hours per day, W/P is goods per labor hour, K is real-capital units, A is an efficiency index, and Y is goods per day. All numerical examples have zero labor and consumption taxes and fixed population/leisure preferences. N* denotes clearing hours; Y* is output at those hours, not an absolute physical ceiling. No AD/SRAS model is introduced.

Shared numerical baseline with Module 8: Y = A × K^0.3 × N^0.7; labor demand W/P = MPN = 0.7 × A × (K/N)^0.3. The illustrative linear supply is W/P = b + 0.05N. Baseline A=10,K=100,b=2 yields N*=100,W/P=7,Y=1000,ALP=10. The permanent improvement is A=11. Holding PVLR fixed for a hypothetical reference keeps b=2. Assumed higher-PVLR cases use b=2.2,2.7,3.1, giving approximately N*=106.93,100,94.59 and real wages 7.55,7.70,7.83. The intercepts illustrate relative resource-response strengths; they do not estimate lifetime wealth or derive a full household utility model.

Channels and timing: Higher productivity shifts demand right. At fixed PVLR, a wage response moves along supply. A permanent resource gain shifts supply left. These channels are separated for explanation, not asserted to occur as two successive shocks. Each case resets to the original baseline. The temporary comparison is a counterfactual reference, not an actual stage the permanent economy must visit. Current reward and lifetime resources are separate objects.

Output and source conflict: SRC-083 p.8, More on Technology, explicitly argues that Y rises after the permanent productivity improvement even if the income effect reduces N, relying on the higher aggregate income behind that effect. SRC-012 slide54 also lists higher A as a rightward LRAS shifter. The older generated Module9 outline called Y ambiguous from A rising and N falling alone; that omitted this course-specific resource interpretation. This lesson follows the instructor sources, and all its illustrative Y values rise. The exact-offset case gives a transparent 10% increase in Y and ALP from A10→11 with N fixed. A generic arbitrarily enormous supply shift is not substituted for the course’s resource interpretation.

Checks and empirical evidence: Practice questions here are generated, not predictions of actual exam questions. Slide34’s empirical observations are not imposed as universal equality of income and substitution effects. Lower clearing hours do not by themselves establish cyclical unemployment. Only stated model assumptions justify the output or unemployment conclusion.
