Model: With w = W/P, labor demand is w = 18 − 0.2N. Baseline labor supply is N = 5(w − 2). Tax scenarios use N = 5(qw − 2) + I, with q = (1 − tₙ)/(1 + t꜀). Here P is the goods price before consumption tax, tₙ is the labor-income tax rate, t꜀ is the consumption tax rate, and I is an illustrative income-effect shift in hours. I is zero in the temporary scenario. The combined tax expression is supplemental accounting. The tax is on the reward from an extra hour; example rates are flat for clarity.

Assumptions: Productivity A, capital K and other stated shifters are fixed. Temporary tax changes have negligible effects on lifetime resources in the course model. From individuals to the market: Aggregate labor supply adds each person’s offered hours at a given wage.
