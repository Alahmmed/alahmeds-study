Notation and assumptions: y and yᶠ are current and future disposable real income; a is opening net assets; c and cᶠ are consumption. The superscript f labels the future. The carried balance a + y − c is a stock; y − c is new saving in the current tax-net period. A negative carried balance represents debt. Zero ending assets and no inheritance give cᶠ = (a + y − c)(1 + r) + yᶠ. The present-value budget is c + cᶠ/(1 + r) = a + y + yᶠ/(1 + r). Initial assets count once.

Preferred versus affordable: Our simple equal-consumption examples use two equal-length periods, known incomes, r = 0, β = 1, identical concave preferences, free borrowing and lending, and zero final assets. Then c = cᶠ = (a + y + yᶠ)/2. Affordability alone does not choose this allocation. With other interest rates or preferences, consumption need not be equal.
